Project Overview

Streamlining Quote-to-Cash Workflows in Enterprise ERP Systems

This foundational research focused on the quote-to-cash journey for Premier One, VESTA, and MV products. The work was designed to uncover why customers were experiencing long-standing friction across contracting, ordering, deployment, and billing, then translate those findings into clear priorities for product and operational improvement.

The challenge

What needed to be understood

Customers had been reporting dissatisfaction across the quote-to-cash process for years, but the organization lacked a shared view of where the breakdowns were happening and why. Contracting, invoicing, order changes, deployment handoffs, and renewal workflows were all contributing to friction, yet the experience had never been mapped holistically.

What the research needed to deliver

  • Map the full journey across touchpoints, systems, and teams.
  • Identify the most severe and recurring pain points.
  • Connect operational delays to lived customer experience.
  • Turn findings into product and process recommendations leadership could act on quickly.

Research Methods

I used a mixed-methods approach so the team could understand not only what was happening in the workflow, but how those breakdowns were showing up in customer experience.

Operational evidence

  • Quantitative analysis: Investigated sales and supply chain data to uncover process insights, contract lag, and the stages that took the longest.
  • Workflow diagnostics: Used system and operational patterns to frame where friction was likely accumulating before qualitative work went deeper.

Direct stakeholder input

  • In-depth interviews: Conducted one-on-one conversations to explore experiences, behaviors, and root causes in detail.
  • Focus groups: Facilitated shared discussions to gather multiple perspectives and understand how cross-functional dynamics shaped the experience.

Pattern validation

  • User surveys: Collected broader, quantifiable input to validate emerging themes and spot trends quickly.
  • Synthesis and prioritization: Organized findings into journey stages, pain point clusters, and opportunity areas the team could act on.

Findings & Insights

The research made it clear that dissatisfaction was not tied to a single product issue. It came from accumulated friction across the quote-to-cash workflow, especially where systems, teams, and billing structures failed to align.

Contract and invoice complexity was a primary pain point

A one-to-many relationship between contracts and invoices created confusion, administrative burden, and ongoing dissatisfaction for customers managing multiple active agreements.

Invoicing and billing were the weakest touchpoints

Customers consistently rated invoicing and billing as the least satisfactory part of the experience, especially around accuracy, timeliness, and interpretability.

Order and deployment handoffs amplified friction

Difficult quoting systems, manual workarounds, and siloed product teams created instability between ordering and deployment, often forcing change orders or rework.

Cycle times varied, but implementation often dragged longest

The duration data showed that implementation was frequently the slowest and most painful stage, making delays more visible to customers and more expensive for the business.

Learning & Reflection

This project reinforced how important it is to frame enterprise workflow research at the right level of abstraction. The quote-to-cash journey touched so many teams, systems, and business rules that the real challenge was not just collecting data. It was creating a structure that made those interdependencies understandable.

Looking back, I would scope the journey content even more selectively from the start, especially around the duration metric and how it should be visualized. I would also think more critically about whether focus groups were the best fit, since they introduced additional complexity during analysis.

The work also underscored how valuable it is to bring quantitative partners in earlier. Tying the operational signals to the qualitative experience was one of the most useful parts of the project, and even more alignment up front would have made that synthesis stronger and faster.
